# One column's purity search eats fourteen minutes

The ethyl-acetate plant still takes about 14 minutes because of one column, SCDS15, and within it a small number of calls, not because warm starts failed in general. SCDS15 takes about 80 % of the run in both timing runs: 654 of 816 s in the coordinator's run, and 726 of 894 s in the build's own benchmark ([build decision 2026-11-25-03](https://github.com/FaireDose/Fugacity/blob/9eb23123d7580e4d4657805e5109de867dfef327/docs/decisions/2026-11-25-03-solve-speed-build.md)). That averages about 6 s per call. A restarted call costs **0.2-0.3 s** when the feed hardly moved, but **13-17 s** when the feed moved by 3-10 %. The reason is how CHEPTA meets SCDS15's 99.99 mass % water purity. It wraps an outer search around complete trial columns, each solved at a fixed distillate flow. A fixed-distillate column of a sharp split restarts badly after a feed change, so those trial columns drop back to the slow cold start. Two things are missing compared with established practice. The first is the purity written as one of the column's own equations (the ChemSep formulation), which CHEPTA uses today only for decanter columns. In a scratch test it converged restarted SCDS15 in **one column of 2-4 Newton iterations (0.14-0.31 s)** for feed changes up to 10 %, with the same distillate within about 2e-10. The second is a cheaper way to decide which stages hold two liquids. Today CHEPTA runs a full stability test on every stage at every derivative step, and that makes the column 2.7-3.4 times dearer than a vapour-liquid one. The case itself is also unusually hard. It has five tear streams, three two-liquid columns, an ethanol inventory that settles very slowly, and a design-spec bracket that ends where a second liquid appears. On full versus loose intermediate solves: other simulators solve every block fully at every pass, and loosening the columns would gain little here. The established way to stop re-solving columns at every pass is a different architecture (simultaneous-modular), not looser tolerances. My estimate is that the purity change alone would bring the run to **3-5 minutes**. Those numbers come from single-column runs, not from a flowsheet run.

## Four nested solvers multiply into 115 calls of every column

Four solvers sit inside one another. Brent's method varies the ethanol feed F1 to meet 0.04 mass % ethanol in the product, and every trial value is a complete recycle solve: 7 solves on the build. Each recycle solve is a Broyden iteration. (A tear stream is a stream whose value is guessed so that a loop can be calculated in sequence. Broyden adjusts the guesses using an estimate of how the loop responds, called the Jacobian, which it updates as it goes.) The loop here is one connected set of 17 blocks with **5 tear streams × 4 components = 20 tear variables** ([flowsheet.js:567](https://github.com/FaireDose/Fugacity/blob/9eb23123d7580e4d4657805e5109de867dfef327/src/flowsheet/flowsheet.js#L567)). When Broyden stops making progress for 8 passes, CHEPTA rebuilds the Jacobian by finite differences. It perturbs each of the 20 tear variables in turn, and each perturbation is a full pass that recalculates every column ([flowsheet.js:653](https://github.com/FaireDose/Fugacity/blob/9eb23123d7580e4d4657805e5109de867dfef327/src/flowsheet/flowsheet.js#L653)). The build's 7 solves took 1, 1, 7, 7, 12, 14 and 13 regular passes, which is 55. The recorded 115 calculations of each block then fit exactly with three such 20-pass rebuilds, one in each of the three longest solves. That split is inferred from the arithmetic; the decision file does not print it.

Inside SCDS15 there are two more levels. Its bottoms purity is met by an outer Newton search on the distillate flow, with step halving and a 15-point scan when Newton fails. Every trial point of that search is a whole column ([specifications.js:17-30](https://github.com/FaireDose/Fugacity/blob/9eb23123d7580e4d4657805e5109de867dfef327/src/units/columns/specifications.js#L17)). Each trial column is then solved by Naphtali-Sandholm, which is Newton's method on all stage equations at once: the MESH equations (material balances, equilibrium, summations, heat balances). CHEPTA builds that Newton Jacobian by central differences, two property calls per stage variable ([engine.js:611-636](https://github.com/FaireDose/Fugacity/blob/9eb23123d7580e4d4657805e5109de867dfef327/src/units/columns/engine.js#L611)). Because the column is declared `VLL`, each of those property calls decides the stage's liquid phases with Michelsen's tangent-plane test. That is a search for a second liquid that would lower the Gibbs energy. Here it uses 8 trial starts of up to 400 iterations, each iteration calling the activity model ([stability.js:72-82](https://github.com/FaireDose/Fugacity/blob/9eb23123d7580e4d4657805e5109de867dfef327/src/equilibrium/stability.js#L72)). The column keeps only one cached result per stage ([two-liquids.js:230](https://github.com/FaireDose/Fugacity/blob/9eb23123d7580e4d4657805e5109de867dfef327/src/units/columns/two-liquids.js#L230)). From the code, that comes to about 11 tests per stage per Newton iteration, which is about 22,000 activity-coefficient calls per iteration of SCDS15.

## Most of SCDS15's time is a few calls that fall back to cold trial columns

The research team ran SCDS15 alone on the build's source, with stream S28 of the converged plant as feed. The script is `scratchpad/r269/col15.mjs`; these are unpublished single-column runs. They show how strongly the cost of one call depends on how far the feed moved since the last call:

| SCDS15 call (VLL, purity spec) | Trial columns | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Restart, feed changed 1e-5 to 1e-4 relative | 2 | 0.28-0.34 s |
| Restart, feed changed 1e-3 | 4 | 1.0-2.9 s |
| Restart, feed changed 1 % | 4-5 | 5.4-7.0 s |
| Restart, feed changed 3-10 % | 7-9 | 13.3-16.6 s |
| Cold start | 13 | 24.6 s |
| Same, purity among the MESH equations (scratch patch), feed changed 1e-5 to 10 % | 1 (2-4 Newton iterations) | 0.14-0.31 s |

The mechanism is the following. A column with a fixed distillate flow and a sharp split is very sensitive to its feed: if D is held while the feed changes, the composition front has to move far along the stages. The measured restart of a fixed-D column converged in 2-4 iterations for feed changes up to 1e-3. At 1 % it stalled ("the residual fell only from 0.001144 to 0.0009603 in 4 iterations"), and the code then dropped to the bubble-point start, which costs about 2 s with the two-liquid tests. The outer search needs several such trial columns. If the restarted search fails as a whole, it starts again from the cold distillate estimate. On main that estimate was 144.9 mol/s against an answer of 111 mol/s ([specifications.js:263-272](https://github.com/FaireDose/Fugacity/blob/9eb23123d7580e4d4657805e5109de867dfef327/src/units/columns/specifications.js#L263)). Percent-level feed jumps do happen in this loop, because Broyden steps of up to 0.62 of a tear's flow are recorded ([flowsheet.js:622](https://github.com/FaireDose/Fugacity/blob/9eb23123d7580e4d4657805e5109de867dfef327/src/flowsheet/flowsheet.js#L622)). An average of 6.3 s per call against 0.3 s for a good restart therefore means that **perhaps 20-30 of the 115 calls cost 15-30 s each** (an estimate). If no call had fallen back, the run would have taken 2-4 minutes: 55 restarted passes at 1-2 s, 60 finite-difference passes at 0.5-1 s, and the 37 s cold first pass.

The proposal's own profile says where the time goes inside those calls. **59 % of SCDS15's time is the stability test of stages that hold one liquid**, 47 % is UNIQUAC's own time, and 15 % is IAPWS-IF97 water enthalpies; the shares overlap, since the stability tests call UNIQUAC ([proposal 0027](https://github.com/FaireDose/Fugacity/blob/e87927f70b08689773395aad5819db7a6640b72e/proposals/0027-solve-speed.md)). At the converged F1, SCDS15 has no two-liquid stage at all. It needs `VLL` only because a second liquid forms on stage 1 at the low end of the design spec's range (6,503.5 kg/h). With `VL` the same run takes 96 s, but the design spec then stops at that boundary ([proposal 0027](https://github.com/FaireDose/Fugacity/blob/e87927f70b08689773395aad5819db7a6640b72e/proposals/0027-solve-speed.md)).

## Two things other simulators do differently: the purity among the column equations, and fewer stability tests

The first gap is how a product purity is specified. The ChemSep Book lists "the mole fraction of a given component in either the distillate or bottoms product stream" among the specification equations, so the purity replaces one equation of the column itself ([ChemSep Book, 2nd ed., sec. 13.2.4](http://www.chemsep.org/book/docs/book2.pdf)). CHEPTA already does this, with DWSIM's `Log(xc/spval)` row, but only for "a column with a decanter and one purity of its bottoms". Every other column goes to the outer loop ([specifications.js:55-71](https://github.com/FaireDose/Fugacity/blob/9eb23123d7580e4d4657805e5109de867dfef327/src/units/columns/specifications.js#L55); the refusal is at [engine.js:376](https://github.com/FaireDose/Fugacity/blob/9eb23123d7580e4d4657805e5109de867dfef327/src/units/columns/engine.js#L376)). That scope was a deliberate decision. When it was taken, decanter columns F9-F12 converged with the row in 11-17 iterations where the outer loop refused ([decision 2026-11-18-07](https://github.com/FaireDose/Fugacity/blob/9eb23123d7580e4d4657805e5109de867dfef327/docs/decisions/2026-11-18-07-purity-in-mesh.md)). The outer loop has a real advantage: when a purity cannot be reached, it can report the most the column can reach, because continuation starts from a column without separation where a purity cannot be met ([specifications.js:34-53](https://github.com/FaireDose/Fugacity/blob/9eb23123d7580e4d4657805e5109de867dfef327/src/units/columns/specifications.js#L34)). The scratch test confirms both sides. From a restart the purity row pins the composition front, so the last profile stays close and Newton converges in 2-4 iterations. From a cold bubble-point start the row did not converge for SCDS15 (5.5 s lost), and the outer loop then ran as before. A hybrid therefore follows: the purity row whenever a restart profile exists, and the outer loop for the first cold call or on failure.

The second gap is how often the two-liquid question is asked. The ChemSep Book notes that in a three-phase column "there will almost certainly be a number (probably a majority) of stages with only one liquid phase present" ([ChemSep Book, sec. 13.7.1](http://www.chemsep.org/book/docs/book2.pdf)). Published methods avoid testing every stage at every evaluation in three ways. The mixed-K formulation of Schuil-Bool and Baden-Michelsen folds both liquids into averaged activity coefficients, so the equation count never changes ([ChemSep Book, sec. 13.7.2](http://www.chemsep.org/book/docs/book2.pdf)). Ottenbacher and Hasse carry both the three-phase and two-phase equations and switch a stage when a liquid flow would turn negative. They report that this "worked very reliable and stable while requiring much less calculation effort than a fully-fledged phase stability test" ([Ottenbacher and Hasse 2006, p. 843-844](https://skoge.folk.ntnu.no/prost/proceedings/distillation06/CD-proceedings/paper082.pdf)). Compositional reservoir simulators skip stability tests where the state moved little since the last test, the "shadow region" method ([DTU Orbit record](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/speeding-up-compositional-reservoir-simulation-through-an-efficie/); [US 7,548,840](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/9e/b7/21/362a2e2b0b2b6a/US7548840.pdf)). None of the sources opened gives a measured speed-up inside a column solver. The CHEPTA profile, with 59 % of the time in tests of one-liquid stages, is the best evidence of what is at stake. Any skipping scheme must still re-test every stage at convergence, because the one-liquid ("trivial") solution always satisfies the MESH equations. CHEPTA's final check ([two-liquids.js:382-390](https://github.com/FaireDose/Fugacity/blob/9eb23123d7580e4d4657805e5109de867dfef327/src/units/columns/two-liquids.js#L382)) already does this.

Elsewhere CHEPTA is in line with established practice. Warm starts from a block's own last profile are the default in Aspen Plus ("Use Results from Previous Convergence Pass") and in COCO/ChemSep. COCO notes the same caveat CHEPTA meets, that old results "can sometimes result in a column not being solved" ([Aspen Plus 10.1 User Guide vol. 2, p. 22-11](https://user.eng.umd.edu/~nsw/chbe446/AspenPlusV10UserGuide2.pdf); [ChemSep Book, sec. 3.1-3.2](http://www.chemsep.org/book/docs/book2.pdf)). Newton on the MESH equations is "generally fast and reliable" and "much less sensitive to difficulties associated with nonideal solutions than are tearing methods" ([ChemSep Book, sec. 13.4](http://www.chemsep.org/book/docs/book2.pdf)). CHEPTA's stage-local finite differences are cheaper than DWSIM's dense, column-wide ones, which re-evaluate every stage for every perturbed variable ([DWSIM NewtonRaphson.vb](https://github.com/DanWBR/dwsim/blob/windows/DWSIM.UnitOperations/UnitOperations/RigorousColumnSolvers/NewtonRaphson.vb)). ChemSep goes one step further with analytic derivatives, which Michelsen and Baden showed to be "well worth while" ([ChemSep Book, sec. 13.4.4](http://www.chemsep.org/book/docs/book2.pdf)). That gain is smaller here, because Newton iterations are 17 % of SCDS15's time. Whether commercial simulators use inside-out rather than Newton by default could not be checked in an open source.

## This plant is demanding by construction, not by accident

CHEPTA's flowsheet test suite tops out at Cavett's problem, four flash drums at fixed temperature and pressure with three recycles, which Broyden solves in 37 iterations ([FLOWSHEET_TESTS.md](https://github.com/FaireDose/Fugacity/blob/9eb23123d7580e4d4657805e5109de867dfef327/docs/FLOWSHEET_TESTS.md)). Lab's plant has five tears around a reactor, an extractor and three rigorous two-liquid columns of 8, 18 and 35 stages. SCDS11 holds two liquids on 32 of its 35 stages. One column carries its own purity spec, and a flowsheet design spec sits on top ([Lab case](https://github.com/FaireDose/chepta-lab/tree/main/cases/ethyl-acetate-piotrowski-2021)). Three-phase distillation with recycles is a known hard class. Ottenbacher and Hasse report that commercial simulators "including Aspen Plus" and plain Newton failed on lab-scale three-phase columns with three recycles ([Ottenbacher and Hasse 2006](https://skoge.folk.ntnu.no/prost/proceedings/distillation06/CD-proceedings/paper082.pdf)).

Two features make this case slower than its size suggests. First, the ethanol balance is nearly integrating. About 0.25 kmol/h of excess ethanol is fed, against roughly 217 kmol/h circulating in S21 alone, so each pass corrects the inventory only slightly. That is why Broyden stalled and needed a 20-pass Jacobian in each long solve. An earlier closed-plant run "gained 0.24 kmol/h of ethanol and lost 0.42 of ester per pass for 200 passes" ([flowsheet.js:879-881](https://github.com/FaireDose/Fugacity/blob/9eb23123d7580e4d4657805e5109de867dfef327/src/flowsheet/flowsheet.js#L879)). It also makes the purity very sensitive to the feed: "5 kg/h less ethanol feed gives 99.50 %" ([Lab README](https://github.com/FaireDose/chepta-lab/tree/main/cases/ethyl-acetate-piotrowski-2021)). Second, the design spec's bracket ends at 6,503.5 kg/h, where SCDS15's stage 1 forms a second liquid. Restarts are hardest there, and solve 6 at that point was the longest (274 s).

## Intermediate solves should stay tight; the real shortcut lies elsewhere

Today nothing is loosened at intermediate steps. Every design-spec evaluation is a full recycle solve to 1e-5. Every column call, including the 60 finite-difference passes, meets 1e-10 in every residual group ([engine.js:192](https://github.com/FaireDose/Fugacity/blob/9eb23123d7580e4d4657805e5109de867dfef327/src/units/columns/engine.js#L192)). SCDS15's purity is met to about 1e-10 in mass fraction every time ([specifications.js:94-96](https://github.com/FaireDose/Fugacity/blob/9eb23123d7580e4d4657805e5109de867dfef327/src/units/columns/specifications.js#L94)). This matches documented practice. Aspen tells users that "inner loops are calculated more accurately than outer loops; otherwise the outer loops would be overwhelmed with errors from inner loops". For optimization it sets the outer tolerance to the square root of the block tolerance, for example 1e-3 outside and 1e-6 inside ([Aspen Plus 10.1 User Guide, p. 17-21, 22-11](https://user.eng.umd.edu/~nsw/chbe446/AspenPlusV10UserGuide2.pdf)).

Solving loosely early and tightly at the end has a theory behind it. Inexact Newton methods let the inner solve be loose in proportion to the outer residual (a "forcing term"), and the choice of that term "is critical to the efficiency of the method and can affect robustness" ([Eisenstat and Walker 1996](https://doi.org/10.1137/0917003)). No simulator was found that applies it to columns or tears, so it would be CHEPTA's own design. In this case it would also gain little. With Newton's quadratic convergence, the gap between 1e-5 and 1e-10 is roughly one Newton iteration out of 2-4 per restarted column, plus perhaps one trial column of SCDS15's search. Those are estimates. It could not be applied to the finite-difference passes at all: column noise of 1e-10 against a tear perturbation of 1e-7 already gives a derivative error of about 1e-3, and a column loosened to 1e-7 would turn those Jacobians into noise. Loosening the recycle itself is worse here. Lab recorded an answer at a recycle tolerance of 3e-4 that "had not settled: the ethanol inventory was still rising" and was off by 0.2 points of purity ([Lab README](https://github.com/FaireDose/chepta-lab/tree/main/cases/ethyl-acetate-piotrowski-2021)).

So the columns do need to be solved fully at every pass, as long as CHEPTA runs a sequential-modular loop. The established way to avoid that is architectural. Simultaneous-modular methods, developed at MIT around 1980 by Boston, Britt, Evans and students, converge the flowsheet on linear models of each column's outlets fitted from rigorous solves. The rigorous column is re-run only to correct its model ([Jirapongphan 1980, MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/99552)). Aspen also offers converging tears and design specs together in one Broyden block, rather than one nested inside the other, and staging the tears before the specs with "Tear Tolerance Ratio" ([Aspen Plus 10.1 User Guide](https://user.eng.umd.edu/~nsw/chbe446/AspenPlusV10UserGuide2.pdf)). No open source opened here quantifies the speed-up of either approach. One smaller point: CHEPTA's warning on this case advised loosening the design-spec tolerance to 0.001. That is larger than the 0.0004 target itself, because the 100× rule is applied to an absolute mass fraction ([design-specs.js:261-271](https://github.com/FaireDose/Fugacity/blob/9eb23123d7580e4d4657805e5109de867dfef327/src/flowsheet/design-specs.js#L261)). The advice should be stated relative to the target.

## Recommendations in order of expected gain

The gains below are estimates from single-column runs and the recorded totals. None of the changes has been run in the flowsheet. "Changes answers" means whether the converged plant differs beyond the tolerances already in force.

| # | Change | Expected gain | Changes answers? | Practice or own design |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bottoms purity as a MESH row for columns with a total condenser and a fixed reflux ratio whenever a restart profile exists; outer loop for the cold first call and on failure ([specifications.js:296](https://github.com/FaireDose/Fugacity/blob/9eb23123d7580e4d4657805e5109de867dfef327/src/units/columns/specifications.js#L296), [engine.js:376](https://github.com/FaireDose/Fugacity/blob/9eb23123d7580e4d4657805e5109de867dfef327/src/units/columns/engine.js#L376)) | Largest: SCDS15 from 654-726 s to about 50-60 s; whole run about 3-5 min | No: same D within about 2e-10 in the scratch test | The formulation is established (ChemSep spec equations; CHEPTA's own decanter row). The restart-first hybrid is CHEPTA's arrangement. It widens decision 2026-11-18-07, so the maintainer decides |
| 2 | Cheaper two-liquid tests: a second cache slot per stage for the base point, and reuse of a clearly stable verdict (tangent-plane minimum well above zero, `tm` is already returned) at perturbed points; the full test at convergence stays | Large on every VLL column: up to the 59 % of SCDS15 spent testing one-liquid stages, plus part of SCDS11's 108 s and SCDS5's 48 s (not measured) | No, if the margin is safe and the final check is kept | Skipping tests in kind is established (shadow region, negative-flow switching, mixed-K); the margin rule is CHEPTA's own |
| 3 | When a restarted purity search fails, restart its cold fallback from the last answer's D, not the cold estimate, and make its first column "quick" ([specifications.js:263-272](https://github.com/FaireDose/Fugacity/blob/9eb23123d7580e4d4657805e5109de867dfef327/src/units/columns/specifications.js#L263)) | Large on fallback calls; mostly redundant once (1) is in | No, within tolerance | CHEPTA's own; proposal 0027 kept "the search of today" on purpose |
| 4 | Design spec: secant from the first two points instead of doubling 1 %-of-range steps; warm-start each solve from the nearest solved point, not the last ([design-specs.js:484-517](https://github.com/FaireDose/Fugacity/blob/9eb23123d7580e4d4657805e5109de867dfef327/src/flowsheet/design-specs.js#L484)) | Medium: solves 3-6 (the bracket walk 6,510.5 → 6,503.5 kg/h, inferred) cost 619 of 894 s | The final F1 moves within the design-spec tolerance | Secant is Aspen's default for one design spec; keeping restarts per solved point is CHEPTA's own |
| 5 | Case side: tear three streams (S6, S10, S24) instead of five, 12 tear variables instead of 20 | Small to medium: finite-difference passes 60 → 36 | Same within the tear tolerance; how Broyden converges with them is unknown | Standard modelling choice; a change to Lab's case, not the code |
| 6 | Analytic or reused stage derivatives inside the column Newton (vapour columns analytic for the ideal vapour; chord steps) | Small: Newton iterations are 17 % of SCDS15's time | No | Established (ChemSep analytic derivatives) |
| 7 | Column tolerance tied to the recycle's progress, full 1e-10 in finite-difference passes and the last pass | Small: about one Newton iteration of 2-4 per restarted column | Intermediate values change; final within tolerance if the last pass is tight | CHEPTA's own (inexact-Newton theory, no simulator found); risk that Broyden sees column noise |
| 8 | Looser recycle tolerance during bracketing | Not recommended | Yes, biased: Lab's 3e-4 answer was wrong | Aspen-style staging exists, but this plant's slow ethanol inventory makes it unsafe |

Recommendations 1 and 2 address the two halves of the measured cost: how many cold trial columns SCDS15 runs, and how expensive each property call is on a two-liquid stage. Both are worth doing, but their gains overlap: (1) removes many of the trial columns whose stability tests (2) would make cheaper. Recommendation 1 needs a decision and tests at the bracket's phase boundary (6,503.5 kg/h, two liquids on stage 1), where the scratch test did not go. Simultaneous-modular convergence or one combined tear-and-spec block is the established route to stop re-solving columns at every pass. It is a proposal-sized redesign with no open benchmark of its gain, and it is worth considering only after 1 and 2 show what floor is left.

## Conclusion

The warm starts of proposal 0027 worked where the feed moved little. The remaining time comes from a formulation choice: posing a high-purity split as a sequence of fixed-distillate columns. That is the poorly conditioned way to pose it, and it turns every percent-level recycle step into a near-cold solve. The fix that other simulators' formulations point to is a better-posed column, not a looser one: the purity among the column's own equations, with the outer loop kept as the safety net it was designed to be. The work on tolerances goes the other way. CHEPTA's tight inner tolerances are correct practice and, in this slow-settling plant, also necessary. What remains open, and what one flowsheet run with recommendation 1 would settle, is whether the measured single-column restarts carry over to the plant's real Broyden steps and to the two-liquid boundary at the bracket's end.
