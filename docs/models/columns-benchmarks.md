# Column benchmarks: the cases and their references

The cases the column engine must pass ([proposal 0015](../../proposals/0015-separation-units.md), milestones M1 and
M2a, steps 2 and 3; workstream W7), written before the engine exists, with the independent calculations that check it. The engine
(`src/units/columns/`, W2) compares itself with them as each of its tasks lands; until then
`test/benchmark-columns.test.js` checks the cases and the references only. docs/BENCHMARKS.md is generated by
`validation/python/benchmark_pairs.py` and lists the benchmark processes and their pairs, not unit cases, so the
column cases are described here.

| Where | What |
|---|---|
| `validation/data/columns/*.json` | one case per file: the column, its thermodynamics and why that method, the specifications, the expected behaviour and outcome, the tolerances, the branch where there could be more than one solution; conventions in its README |
| `validation/python/reference_mesh.py` | the independent equilibrium-stage solution (MESH, all equations together, numpy and scipy) and the checks with exact answers; writes `validation/fixtures/columns.json` |
| `validation/python/reference_fug.py` | the shortcut: Fenske, Underwood, Gilliland in Eduljee's form, the feed stage |
| `test/benchmark-columns.test.js` | the cases are well formed; every stage's balances of the reference close, recomputed in JavaScript (with CHEPTA's own NRTL, vapour pressures and enthalpies for ethanol + water); the shortcut's equations hold at the fixture's numbers |

Regenerate the fixtures: `python validation/python/reference_mesh.py` (about fifteen minutes: the two-liquid cases split every stage's liquid at every evaluation) and `python validation/python/reference_hydraulics.py`.

## The complete benchmark: The ChemSep Book, benzene + toluene (B2)

**Source.** H. A. Kooijman and R. Taylor, *The ChemSep Book*, 2nd edition (2006), public domain
(http://www.chemsep.org/downloads/docs/book2.pdf, read 2026-10-29): section 1.2 (pp. 9-17) sets the column up,
section 1.3 (pp. 18-20) prints its results as screenshots of ChemSep's tables: Figure 1.19 (temperatures, pressure,
flows of the ten stages), Figure 1.20 (benzene's x, y and K), Figure 1.21 (feed and products), Figure 1.18 (balances).
They are sharp text, transcribed into the case's `published` block with where each number is.

**Search** (2026-10-29), for an open worked example with stage results: the DWSIM tutorial (B1's setup, no
converged values); The ChemSep Book (this example; also a depropanizer and an extractive column, nonequilibrium,
section 2); textbooks with such examples (Seader, Henley and Roper; Wankat; Kister) are not open.

**Setup** as published: 1.1 atm (111.4575 kPa) everywhere, 10 stages counted with the total condenser (1) and the
partial reboiler (10), feed on stage 5, equimolar, saturated liquid, 11.7471 mol/s, reflux ratio 2, bottoms
5.874 mol/s, efficiency 1, no heat loss.

**Two comparisons, kept apart.**

1. *Numerical:* the engine on CHEPTA's method (Peng–Robinson for both phases, k_ij = 0 as the databank has none for
   the pair, energy balances; METHOD_SELECTION rules 2 and 6: nonpolar hydrocarbons) against `reference_mesh.py`,
   within the numerical tolerances below.
2. *Agreement with the published example:* the published numbers come from another property method (UNIFAC,
   ChemSep's Antoine equations, constant molar overflow, p. 13) solved to a norm of 1e-3. CHEPTA's method is within
   0.85 K and 0.018 in benzene's mole fraction of them on every stage (0.73 K and 0.013 with constant molar overflow;
   CHEPTA's K of benzene runs from 1.0 % above the published y/x at the top to 0.6 % below it at the bottom, so the
   relative volatility differs). NRTL with the ChemSep set came within 0.37 K and 0.0059. The tolerances, 1.0 K and
   0.02, state agreement between two property methods, not the accuracy of either against measured data.

**What the transcription test checks** (`test/benchmark-columns.test.js`): the published numbers agree with each
other to their printed rounding: D + B = F, the component balances, x_D and x_B from the product flows, the reflux and
boil-up ratios, and the operating lines of constant molar overflow from each stage's liquid to the vapour of the stage
below. One thing does not: the K column of Figure 1.20 is 0.03 % to 2.8 % above y/x of the same figure (stage 1:
y/x = 1.0491, K = 1.07974); x and y are consistent with the flows, so they are compared and K is not.

## The further M2a cases

- **F1, two liquids (1-butanol + water, NRTL, the ChemSep set, which predicts the split):** the reference solves the
  column with one liquid and tests each stage's liquid by the tangent-plane distance, tpd(w) = Σ w_i [ln w_i +
  ln γ_i(w) − ln x_i − ln γ_i(x)], over 20001 trial compositions: about −0.03 on stages 1 and 2 (two liquids), zero on
  stages 3 to 6. The test recomputes it with CHEPTA's own activity coefficients. The engine must refuse with
  PHASE_SPLIT (outcome unsupported) and name the stages; the one-liquid profile is metastable and never returned.
- **B3, near an azeotrope (acetone + methanol, NRTL fitted to Li et al. 2018):** the model's azeotrope is x = 0.7798 at
  328.31 K (measured, for information: 0.80 at 55.7 °C); the distillate is 0.7786 and the top five stages are within
  0.01 K of each other. From the reference's start Newton's method needs 12 steps (B1: 2).
- **E4, not converged:** no open case is known whose reflux-ratio and flow specifications pass the input checks and
  still have no solution (E1 to E3 are the infeasible ones), so the expected failure is made certain by an iteration
  limit of 1 on the B1 column. The name of the option is W2's.
- **P1, performance:** four components, 40 stages, NRTL, one liquid on every stage; a cold start and a warm start
  (from the cold solution, R from 3 to 3.3), each with its reference profile. W9 sets the budget and the machine; the
  tolerances and the phase check stay as in every case.
- **Several feeds (A4) and the partial condenser (A3)** are in the reference; A4's McCabe-Thiele construction with
  three operating lines equals its profile to 1e-14.

## Milestone M2b: two liquids, the column's own method, hydraulics

Written ahead of W2's and W4's M2b tasks (decision file docs/decisions/2026-10-30-01-m2b-cases.md); the tests check the
cases and the references, the engine's comparison is W2's and W4's.

| F2 decanter (1-butanol + water) | 10 stages, a water-rich feed (0.3 butanol) on stage 2, a decanter refluxing all of the butanol-rich liquid and drawing the water-rich one, bottoms 28.9 mol/s: 0.994 butanol at the bottom; two liquids on stages 1 to 6 | ok |

**Two liquids on a stage** (`ActivityVLL` in `reference_mesh.py`): the stage's liquid of overall composition x is its
stable state at T, one liquid, or two in liquid-liquid equilibrium found by Michelsen's tangent-plane test and a split
(successive substitution with the Rachford-Rice equation, then Newton's method on the equal activities, to 1e-14). The
vapour is in equilibrium with both liquids; the stage's K is written on the overall liquid, K_i = y_i/x_i, and its
liquid enthalpy is the two liquids' — the averaging of The ChemSep Book, section 13.7.2 (public domain), so the MESH
equations are unchanged. In a binary at fixed pressure a stage with vapour and two liquids has no degree of freedom
left, so all of F1's and F2's two-liquid stages share T and the two liquid compositions; F3, a ternary, does not.
The tests check, with CHEPTA's own NRTL, that the two liquids have equal activities, that the overall liquid lies
between them and is unstable, and that every one-liquid stage is stable on a grid of trial liquids.

**The decanter:** the condensate at its bubble point splits; all of the liquid richer in the named component is the
reflux (so the reflux is not a specification), all of the other the distillate. F2 has a second solution with two
liquids on every stage and the bottoms at 0.46 butanol, which also closes every balance: the reference's start puts the
stages below the decanter on the butanol-rich side, and the case names that branch.

**The boundary between two methods** (proposal 0015, Part I, section 3): a stream keeps its component flows, pressure
and enthalpy flow; its temperature (and vapour fraction) in the receiving method is found by a P-H flash, solved here by
brentq on T over a vapour-liquid flash to 1e-10 K. The enthalpies of the two methods at the same T differ (PR's
departure enthalpy against NRTL's liquid heat capacities): 411 J/mol for the feed at 350 K, and the PR distillate at
the NRTL column's 357.9 K would be partly vapour. The fixture's `energy.imbalance_if_recalculated_at_same_T_W` shows
what a same-T recalculation would create. No open worked example of a column with its own method was found; the
checks are the balance and the round trip (a stream into the other method and back returns at its temperature within
1e-11 K).

**Hydraulics** (`validation/data/columns/hydraulics/`, `reference_hydraulics.py`): H1 is the open worked example of
M. Fermeglia, "Staged column design", University of Trieste (2021), slides 44-47 (free to read): pure n-hexane at 69 °C,
ρV = 0.1917 and ρL = 41.12 lb/ft³, σ = 13.2 dyn/cm, L/V = 0.8, 24 in spacing, C_sb = 0.36 ft/s **read off Fair's chart
by the author**, 75 % of flooding, η = 0.90, V = 2500 lbmol/h; printed: F_lv = 0.0546, K = 0.331, u_flood = 4.836 ft/s,
D = 11.03 ft. Every printed number is reproduced from the inputs within 0.09 %. H2 is made so that the result does not
depend on the fitted form of Fair's chart W4 chooses: its stages share F_lv, spacing and σ, so C_sb cancels from the
ratios. Open fitted forms of the chart seen: the University of Oklahoma's ChE 4253 slides (2018, "Flash and column
design exercises", free to read) print two, log10 C_SB = −1.1977 − 0.53143 log10 F_LV − 0.1876 (log10 F_LV)² (PDF page 10)
and −0.94506 − 0.70234 log10 F_LV − 0.22618 (log10 F_LV)² (PDF page 12, its example), without the tray spacing they
belong to; their worked example also has slips (F_LV printed as 0.027 where its data give 0.27; 0.30103 m per ft; A =
A_N/1.2 on PDF page 11 and A_N/0.8 on page 13). They are listed for W4, not used in the cases.

## The thermodynamics of the model systems

A1, A2, A3, D1, E1 and E2 are made-up model systems, not real mixtures: K_i = α_i p(T)/P with a reference vapour
pressure ln(p/kPa) = 16 − 4000/T (it only sets the temperatures), liquid enthalpy 0 and vapour enthalpy 30 kJ/mol for
every component. The energy balances then give constant molar overflow exactly, so McCabe-Thiele, Fenske and Underwood
are exact or nearly so, which is what makes them checks. docs/METHOD_SELECTION.md does not apply to them. The engine
runs them through a test system with these K-values and enthalpies.

B2 uses Peng–Robinson for both phases (nonpolar hydrocarbons, rules 2 and 6). B1, E3 and E4 use NRTL with the default parameter set of ethanol + water (fitted to Kamihama et al. 2012, ThermoML
Archive) and an ideal-gas vapour: rule 3 of docs/METHOD_SELECTION.md (polar, below 10 bar, fitted pairs).

## The equations of the references

- **MESH** per stage: component balances, equilibrium y = K x, the summations of x and y, and the energy balance, with
  the condenser's and the reboiler's duties; a total condenser is not an equilibrium stage (its liquid at its bubble
  point), a partial one is. All unknowns are solved together by Newton's method (finite-difference Jacobian, step
  halving) from a bubble-point start, until every scaled residual is below 1e-12. The equations are the stage balances
  as NeqSim's and DWSIM's open documentation write them; the code is written from first principles.
- **Shortcut**, as DWSIM's open source code (GPL-3.0, `ShortcutColumn.vb`, read 2026-10-29) documents it; the
  original articles are paywalled and not used:
  N_min = ln[(x_D,LK/x_D,HK)(x_B,HK/x_B,LK)] / ln α_LK,HK; the non-keys by d_i/b_i = (d_HK/b_HK)(α_i/α_HK)^N_min;
  Underwood Σ α_i z_i/(α_i − θ) = 1 − q with θ between the keys and R_min = Σ α_i x_D,i/(α_i − θ) − 1; Gilliland in
  Eduljee's form X = (R − R_min)/(R + 1), Y = 0.75 (1 − X^0.5668), N = (Y + N_min)/(1 − Y); the stages below the feed
  N_S = N_min,S N / N_min. Gilliland's correlation is a fit to columns: its N is shown, never compared as a check.

## Tolerances: numerical, kept apart from data

- **Numerical** (the engine against the references): the rigorous column's flows within 1e-6 relative, mole fractions
  within 1e-7, temperatures within 1e-5 K, and its own balances closed on every stage to 1e-8 (proposal 0015,
  Engineering basis); the shortcut within 1e-9 relative (closed formulas and one root).
- **Checks with exact answers** (in the fixture, each with its tolerance and why): McCabe-Thiele within 1e-9; the
  total-reflux limit within 1e-3 at R = 10⁴ (it is 1.1e-4); the rigorous reflux at 101 stages within −0.1 % and +1 % of
  R_min (it is 0.99999 R_min: below by a hair because the rigorous column puts less of the heavy non-key in the
  distillate than Fenske's estimate in the shortcut's x_D).
- **Agreement with measured data**, for information only, never a numerical tolerance: B1's model azeotrope,
  x = 0.8805 at 351.37 K, against the handbook value 0.892 at 78.1 °C (validation/data/azeotropes_101kPa.json). That
  says how good the NRTL pair is, not whether the column is solved.
- **Branches:** the constant-alpha cases have one solution. For B1 the reference's solution, from its stated start, is
  the one to match; another solution that closes every balance is reported for review, not hidden. Underwood's root
  is the one between the keys.
