# 2026-10-29-05: Proposal 0015, milestone M1, W4: the drum and decanter sizes

Started by the maintainer on 2026-10-29 (step 6 of M1). Written against contracts v1 (ARCHITECTURE.md, contract 4).

## What was built

- `src/sizing/sizing.js`: `sizeDrum` (a vertical or horizontal vapour-liquid drum) and `sizeDecanter` (a vertical or
  horizontal decanter for the drum's two liquids), each `(flash result, options) → { dimensions, basis, source, notes }`;
  `flashBlockResult` gives a solved flowsheet's flash block with its outlets. Densities and viscosities from CHEPTA's
  own records at the drum's T and P; a record outside its range refuses. The column diameter and the membrane area
  still refuse (M2b, M3).
- The capability `size_drum` (`src/api/dispatcher.js`), so the command line and the MCP server have it; the size shown
  under a flash drum's results in the Flowsheet workspace, with the defaults (`src/ui/flowsheet-view.js`).
- `validation/python/reference_sizing.py` (the formulas written anew) and `test/sizing.test.js`; docs/models/sizing.md.
- A new kind of source, "course notes": university slides and lecture notes free to read on the university's own site
  (`test/sources.test.js`, `make_sources.py`).

## The sources, and what to check in each

No open textbook with a drum design was found; course slides of three universities and one free trade-journal article
were opened. Please compare each number with the source:

| What | Value in CHEPTA | Where to check |
|---|---|---|
| K fit, vertical drum | A = −1.877478, B = −0.814580, C = −0.187074, D = −0.014523, E = −0.001015; K in ft/s; F = (W_L/W_V)√(ρV/ρL) from 0.006 to 5.5 | Fermeglia, Trieste (2021), slide 83 (and its chart); Bagajewicz, Oklahoma (2013), slide "D related to vapor velocity" |
| security factor | 0.85 of the permitted velocity | Fermeglia, slide 88 |
| heights | h_v = 36 in + ½ feed line, at least 48 in; h_f = 12 in + ½ feed line; 3 < H/D < 5 | Fermeglia, slide 87 (writes ½ D); Bagajewicz (2013) (writes "½ diameter of feedline", taken) |
| feed line | at 60/√ρ_mix ft/s (100/√ρ_mix is the upper limit), ρ_mix in lb/ft³ | Fermeglia, slide 87 |
| liquid hold-up | 2 min | Bagajewicz (2013), slide with h_L: "or ~2 minutes residence time" |
| horizontal drum | K_H = 1.25 K_V; vapour 20 % of the area (15-25 %); vapour space ≥ 20 % of D or 12 in; L/D from 3 | Coker, Oil & Gas Journal, 10 May 1993 |
| decanter | 150 μm drops; settling capped at 4 × 10⁻³ m/s; band 10 % of the height, 2-5 min; hold-up 2-10 min; H = 2D vertical, L = 4D horizontal (3-5); inlet 1 m/s | Al-Bayati, University of Baghdad, "Liquid-liquid separators", pages "Decanter design" and examples 1 and 2 |

Two slips found in the sources, stated in the test and in docs/models/sizing.md: the Oklahoma 2018 worked example takes
log₁₀ in place of ln in the fit (its K = 0.2365 ft/s; with ln, as the chart shows, 0.347 ft/s) and 0.30103 m per ft;
the Baghdad example 2 writes its inlet flow as 2.932 × 10⁻³ m³/s where its numbers give 8.49 × 10⁻³ m³/s.
The chart of slide 83 is Watkins' (1967) as fitted by Blackwell (1984); both are paywalled and were not used.

## Choices of this project (to confirm or change)

- The defaults: 85 % of the permitted velocity, 2 min of liquid, 150 μm drops, the dispersed liquid the one with the
  smaller volume flow, the feed line at the lower nozzle velocity (the taller drum).
- A horizontal drum is at least 3 D long (Coker's starting L/D taken as the least).
- Not checked, for want of an open source opened in this step: the length a horizontal drum needs for the drops to
  fall out of the vapour, a demister, and the Reynolds limit of Stokes' law (the drop Reynolds number is reported).
- Liquid mixture viscosity: ln μ = Σ x ln μ_i, the thermo library's preferred rule (MIT).

## How it was checked

- `test/sizing.test.js`: the formulas against the Python reference within 1e-9; the K fit against its chart read by
  pixel position (within 7 %); the Oklahoma and Baghdad worked examples with their printed numbers; a flash drum
  (ethanol + water, the example project) and a three-phase drum (ethyl acetate + water) end to end, and through the
  dispatcher; the refusals. `npm test` passes.
