# 2026-11-12-05: Acetic acid as a liquid below its triple point (Lab's request 3)

Accepted by the maintainer on 2026-11-12, from CHEPTA-Lab: its ethyl acetate plant feeds acetic acid at 15 degC
(288.15 K), pure and in mixtures with water and ethanol, and CHEPTA refused it (`OUT_OF_RANGE`, records from 289.81 K).

## 1. Which records stop at 289.81 K, and why

| Record | Range before | ChemSep v8.3's own range | Why it stopped at 289.81 K |
|---|---|---|---|
| liquid density | 289.81-594.45 K | 273.15-594.75 K | CHEPTA's cut: `from_chemsep` raises Tmin to the triple point |
| liquid viscosity | 289.81-474 K | 288.15-474 K | the same cut |
| surface tension | 289.81-564.72 K | 288.15-591.95 K | the same cut |
| liquid thermal conductivity | 289.81-391.05 K | 274.09-391.05 K | the same cut |
| liquid heat capacity | 289.81-400 K | **289.69**-400 K | the same cut; ChemSep itself starts at 289.69 K |
| heat of vaporization, vapour viscosity, vapour thermal conductivity | 289.81 K | 289.81 K | ChemSep's own limit (the triple point) |
| vapour pressure | 290-523.15 K | (ChemSep: 273.15 K) | the legacy record fitted to the thermo library's values; its lower end follows McDonald et al. 1959 (290.26-391 K, WebBook) |
| dimerization constant (`association`) | no range stored | | not a limit |

What blocked the flash was the **liquid heat capacity** only (`enthalpy.js`, `hLiquidPure`). A mixture's liquid
enthalpy needs each component's liquid heat capacity, so the acid in a water or ethanol mixture was refused for the same
reason. The vapour-pressure equation is evaluated **without a range check** in an activity-model system
(`system.js` `psat`; `pure().psat` does check): a flash at 288.15 K already used it, about 2 K below its stored range.

  viscosity from 293.15 K, vapour pressure from 298.15 K, liquid heat capacity only 308.15-333.15 K; no enthalpy of

- **Free copies** (`open_copy.py --all`, Unpaywall, OpenAlex, CORE): none for Martin and Andon 1982
  (10.1016/0021-9614(82)90083-0), Steele et al. 1997 (10.1021/je970099y; no supplement either) or Parks and Kelley 1925

  surface tension) were refitted over the wider range with their usual targets (0.21 %, 0.25 %, 0.20 %). The ideal-gas

- **Not extended, refusal stays:** the liquid heat capacity below 289.69 K (so the liquid enthalpy, hence a flash, at
  15 degC). Its record carries `below_range_searched` (the list above), and the refusal prints it and says that a liquid
  at 289.69 K (16.5 degC) or above works. A feed at 16.5 degC or more works; **15 degC still does not**.
- Not changed: the vapour-pressure record. No open measured value below 290.26 K; ChemSep's equation, which reaches
  273.15 K, is 2.0 % lower at 288.15 K than the record, so the two differ by more than a rounding and choosing between

- The scipy reference of the liquid ethyl acetate esterification (`validation/fixtures/reactions.json`, regenerated by
  `reference_reactions.py`; only that entry kept, the vapour entries differ by library versions only) follows the new

## 4. Is the pure liquid's range the right limit in a mixture?

- METHOD_SELECTION.md rule 8 says "no temperature below the melting point of a component that is present (it would
  freeze)". For a dilute component that is wrong as written: acetic acid diluted in water does not freeze at 16.7 degC;
  the mixture's freezing is a solid-liquid equilibrium of the mixture (the Crystallizer has one), far lower.
- What the pure record range does in a mixture is give the **reference state** of the component's liquid enthalpy
  (the hypothetical pure supercooled liquid, with h^E from the activity model). That needs the pure liquid's heat
  capacity below its melting point, an extrapolation of a measured or databank curve into a metastable state.
- **What other simulators do** (METHOD_SELECTION.md, "When in doubt"; read 2026-11-12, at the coordinator's request):
  - **DWSIM** (class library, `DWSIM.Thermodynamics/PropertyPackages/PropertyPackage.vb`, branch `windows`, raw file
    https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DanWBR/dwsim/windows/DWSIM.Thermodynamics/PropertyPackages/PropertyPackage.vb):
    `AUX_LIQ_Cpi` (line 7765) evaluates the compound's liquid heat-capacity correlation through `CalcCSTDepProp`
    (line 9217) at any temperature below the critical temperature: neither function reads a lower or upper temperature
    limit (no clamp, no refusal). Above Tc it substitutes the ideal-gas heat capacity ("surrogate for supercritical
    gases solved in liquid"). With no liquid correlation it estimates one (Rowlinson-Bondi). The melting point is not
    used as a limit. So DWSIM extrapolates silently.
  - **ChemSep** (The ChemSep Book, 2nd ed., 2006, public domain, http://www.chemsep.org/downloads/docs/book2.pdf):
    section 10.2.3, p. 147: "ChemSep uses a temperature correlation for all temperatures to prevent problems arising
    from using different liquid heat capacity methods in the same column"; the mixture liquid heat capacity is "the
    molar average of the component liquid heat capacities". p. 142 (section 10.2): the correlation needs its
    temperature limits, and "if the temperature specified falls out of the temperature range of a correlation ...
    normally an alternative (default) method will be used automatically" (for the other properties, e.g. viscosity,
    p. 149: "if the temperature is within the valid range"). So for the liquid heat capacity ChemSep keeps its own
    correlation outside the range, and its enthalpy reference for a component in a mixture is that pure-liquid curve.
  - **thermo / chemicals** (Caleb Bell, thermo 0.6.1, `thermo.heat_capacity.HeatCapacityLiquid` and
    `thermo.utils.TDependentProperty.T_dependent_property`, source read in this session): each method has `T_limits`;
    outside them, if an extrapolation is set the property is extrapolated, and the heat capacity's `extrapolation` is
    `"linear"` by default (a straight line from the edge value and slope); with none set the result is `None` (or a
    `RuntimeError` when `RAISE_PROPERTY_CALCULATION_ERROR` is on). It does not stop at the melting point.
  - **Where they differ from CHEPTA:** all three evaluate the liquid heat capacity outside the correlation's range and
    none uses the pure melting point as a limit; CHEPTA refuses (AGENTS.md: "fail loudly; never return a silently wrong
    answer"), which none of them does. The difference to keep: CHEPTA's extrapolation must be stated, never silent.

## Decided (the maintainer, 2026-11-12: "we should allow this to happen")

1. **Stated extrapolation of the liquid heat capacity**, built. Below a record's lower end, at most 10 K
   (`EXTRAP_K`, `src/thermo/enthalpy.js`), the heat capacity is the straight line through the record's value and slope at
   its end (as thermo's default); beyond 10 K the liquid enthalpy is refused as before, with `below_range_searched`.
   Every result that uses it carries the warning, e.g. for acetic acid at 288.15 K: "Acetic acid: 288.15 K is 1.54 K
   below its liquid heat-capacity record (289.69-400 K): the heat capacity is extrapolated linearly from the record's
   lower end (its value and slope there), 10 K at most. This is an extrapolation, not data." **General for every
   liquid heat-capacity record, not only acetic acid**: it is one code path (`hLiquidPure`, `cpExt`) and the warning is
   the same text with the component's name, so a limit to one component would only have meant a list to maintain.
   (The anchor of the enthalpy is at or above the record's lower end for every component, so the extrapolation only
   ever reaches below the anchor.)
2. **Vapour pressure in the activity-model mixture path**, built as a **warning only; the refusal beyond 10 K was tried
   and not kept**. The warning (`system.js`, `warnings(T, P)`, for every temperature outside the record, either side):
   "Acetic acid: 288.15 K is 1.85 K below its vapour-pressure record (290-523.15 K): the record's equation is evaluated
   beyond its range. This is an extrapolation, not data." The record is kept; a user's own `psat` is not warned about.
   **Why no refusal:** with a refusal more than 10 K outside the record (in `psat`, then in the final result only) the
   full test failed in 24 places, among them six of the open measured vapour-liquid sets of `validation/data/`, fitted
   and checked at temperatures far below the vapour-pressure record (gamma-butyrolactone at 320.6 K against a record
   starting at 377 K; 1,3-propanediol at 273.15 K against 292.5 K; diethyl ether at 255 K against 270 K), and hot
   reactors above a record's end (methanol at 493 K against 470 K; water at 624 K against 523 K). The records are
   narrower than the data they are used with, so a refusal would stop validated work. **The maintainer decides**
   whether to refuse only below the record by more than 10 K where the temperature is not a measured one, or to widen
   those records first (a data task).
3. **METHOD_SELECTION rule 8** (restate with the mixture's solid-liquid equilibrium): a follow-up proposal, not in this
   pull request. **4** (other components): only on request, component by component.
- Other tests changed **because of the heat-capacity extrapolation** (they asserted a refusal 1 K or 15 K below a record):
  `benchmark-columns` G3 (refusal now 11 K below benzene's record, 278.68 K), `columns-vll` W7 G3 (the feed at 250 K
  instead of the case's 265 K, which is now inside the 10 K: the validation data are untouched; **note** that the case's
  265 K feed is below benzene's triple point of 278.68 K, so the column's NRTL now accepts a 50 % benzene feed colder
  than pure benzene freezes: the maintainer should know), `nonvolatile` (the refusal's wording).
- The test `test/acetic-acid-supercooled.test.js` changed **because of this decision**: before, it asserted the refusal
  at 288.15 K; now it asserts that 288.15 K works, pure and with water, with both warnings, that 275 K is refused naming
  the 10 K limit, that the heat-capacity refusal beyond 10 K names what was searched, and that nothing warns inside the records.

## Decided (the maintainer, 2026-11-13, on #236 as built)

- The vapour pressure outside its record is **warned about only, never refused, for now**.
- **Follow-up (data): widen the vapour-pressure records.** The refusal trial (section "Decided" above, point 2) found
  records narrower than the data and calculations that use them. Components named by the trial's failures: gamma-
  butyrolactone (record 376.99-731 K, used at 320.6 K, set gbl + tetrahydrofuran); 1,3-propanediol (292.5-721 K, used
  at 273.15 K, set 1,3-propanediol + methanol); diethyl ether (270-467.9 K, used at 255 K, sets dichloroethane + diethyl
  ether); at the hot end, methanol (250-470 K, used at 493 K, the equilibrium reactor test) and water (273.16-523.15 K,
  used at 624 K, the adipic acid flowsheet test). The sets dichloroethane + dioxane and 2-butanol + water also failed
  in the trial, without the component being read from the message; the complete list is what the trial gives when run
  again (refuse more than 10 K outside the record in `warnings()`, run `npm test`). Widening follows DATA_RULES (open
  sources, ChemSep taken as it is where it has a wider range).
- **G3 (benzene, a column feed at 265 K, below benzene's triple point 278.68 K):** the column's NRTL accepting it inside
  the 10 K extension is accepted; the case's description (`validation/data/columns/G3-own-method-cannot-represent.json`
  says the NRTL liquid enthalpy refuses below benzene's record) is to be corrected together with the rule-8 proposal.

## 5. For the maintainer (before the decision above; kept for the record)

Check against the source: ChemSep v8.3 (`chemsep1.xml`, the compound with CAS 64-19-7): the lower temperatures of
LiquidDensity 273.15, LiquidViscosity 288.15, SurfaceTension 288.15, LiquidThermalConductivity 274.09,
LiquidHeatCapacityCp 289.69 K; and the WebBook rows named in section 2.

Decide (recommendation first):
1. **15 degC feed. Recommendation changed after the check of other simulators (section 4):** DWSIM, ChemSep and
   thermo all evaluate the liquid heat capacity outside its range, so a refusal here is stricter than any of them. I now
   recommend a **stated extrapolation**: below 289.69 K the heat-capacity curve is continued linearly (as thermo's
   default) for a few kelvin, 10 K at most (the maintainer's precedent for dichloromethane's heat capacity, 2026-10-18),
   with a warning in every result that names the record's end, the distance, and that it is not data
   (`warnings`, the way the heat-capacity record's end is already warned about above its range). Without a measured
   value this changes no tier: the number is labelled extrapolated. The alternative, keeping the refusal, costs
   CHEPTA-Lab its 15 degC feed until an open measurement is found. Not implemented here: it needs the maintainer.
2. **Vapour pressure at 288.15 K:** recommended: keep the record; add a warning (or a refusal) when the activity-model
   system evaluates it outside 290-523.15 K, so the mixture path agrees with `pure().psat`; or replace the legacy
   record by ChemSep's equation (273.15 K, 2 % lower at 288 K), a data-source change for every acetic acid result.
3. **Rule 8 of METHOD_SELECTION.md:** recommended (stronger after section 4: no open simulator uses the pure melting
   point as a limit): restate it as "no temperature at which the mixture's solid-liquid equilibrium puts a solid in"
   (the pure melting point only for a pure component) and propose it as a follow-up proposal; not changed here.
4. **The same extension for other components** (every ChemSep record is cut at the triple point): recommended only on
   request, component by component, when an open source covers it.
