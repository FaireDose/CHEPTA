# 2026-10-22: Reaction description, K(T) and rate forms (proposal 0010, step 2)

Pull request: reactions step 2 (`src/reactions/`).

## What the maintainer asked for

"can go to next" (2026-10-22), after the formation data (step 1) was merged. This is the next step of proposal 0010.

## What was done

- **The reaction description and its checks** (`checkReaction`, `checkReactions`, `reactionBalance`):
  - components are looked up by name, alias, CAS number or formula;
  - the element balance is checked against the components' formulas, and the imbalance is named;
  - the phase (vapour or liquid) sets the standard state;
  - the equilibrium is from the formation data or a cited ln K correlation (ln K = A + B/T + C ln T);
  - the rate law is checked too.
- **ΔrH°, ΔrG° and K(T)** (`reactionProperties`): from the components' formation records by the Gibbs–Helmholtz
  equation with the databank's heat capacities. Records of another state are taken to the reaction's standard state
  by stated paths:
  - ideal gas ⇄ pure liquid through the saturated vapour;
  - crystal → liquid with the melting data.
- **The rate forms** (`reactionRate`, `RATE_FORMS`): power law, reversible power law (reverse rate given, or from K),
  LHHW, Michaelis–Menten, Monod with substrate or product inhibition, and the Blackman, Tessier, Moser and Contois
  equations. Each constant can follow Arrhenius with k0 T^B. The forms are as printed in Rawlings and Ekerdt, 2nd ed.
- **Checks:** validation/python/reference_reactions.py.
  - Vapour reactions against Cantera 3.2.0, with NASA 9 polynomials fitted on 25 K ranges to the same ideal-gas heat
    capacities, and against a scipy enthalpy-and-entropy integration. Cantera and scipy agree to 8e-9 in ln K; the
    engine meets both within 1e-6 relative in K, 298–700 K.
  - Liquid reactions against the scipy route, within 1e-6 in ln K: diethyl ether from ethanol, and glucose to
    fructose from their crystal records.
  - test/reactions.test.js has 12 tests.
- Docs: [docs/models/reactions.md](../models/reactions.md).

## Choices made in this step (for the maintainer to confirm)

1. **Standard pressure 100 kPa** (1 bar, as CODATA and the measured formation records). ChemSep does not state the
   reference pressure of its Gibbs energies of formation in the fields read. If it were 1 atm, the difference would be
   at most RT ln(1.01325) = 33 J/mol per mole of gas in a reaction, 1.3 % in K at 298 K.
2. **Pure-liquid standard state at T.** The effect of pressure is neglected. The liquid enthalpy is on the
   liquid-heat-capacity basis of the mixture enthalpies, with Peng–Robinson for the saturated vapour where a
   component has critical constants (as `pure()` does). An activity-model system with an ideal-gas vapour puts the
   saturated vapour's residual enthalpy at 0 instead. In step 3 the reactor's energy balance will use the system's
   own enthalpies, so a reactor's duty follows the chosen property method.
3. **Crystal records to the liquid** use ΔHfus at Tm with no correction for the heat capacities. This affects the
   sugars, the solid acids and levoglucosan.
4. **Dimerizing acids** (acetic acid): the maintainer, 2026-10-22: "I cannot add reactions for acetic acid? we need
   to fix this in this package". Fixed. The formation record is ChemSep's ideal-gas monomer, and it is taken to the
   liquid through the saturated vapour of monomers and dimers, with the chemical theory CHEPTA already uses
   (`src/thermo/vapour.js`). The fugacity of the saturated vapour is the monomer's partial pressure. Its enthalpy, per
   mole of acid, is y_D ΔH_dim/(y_M + 2 y_D) against the monomer, with ΔH_dim = −R ln10 · B = −60.6 kJ/mol from the
   temperature dependence of the dimerization constant (van 't Hoff; the constant's source is the association record,
   Marek and Standart as used by Schmid et al. 2007). ChemSep's heat of vaporization of acetic acid, 22.8 kJ/mol at
   298.15 K, is to this associated vapour.
   - Check against measured values: acetic acid's liquid ΔfH° from this path is −482.9 kJ/mol; the WebBook's most
     recent value is −483.5 kJ/mol (Steele et al. 1997). Its ΔfG° is −388.3 kJ/mol against −388.1 kJ/mol (with S°
     158.0 J/(mol K), Martin and Andon 1982). Ethanol, ethyl acetate and water are within 1 kJ/mol too.
   - Esterification K on the pure-liquid basis comes out at 23 at 298 K and 18 at 353 K, with ΔrH° = −5.6 kJ/mol.
   - What is still missing: the mixture enthalpies of systems with acetic acid (`src/thermo/enthalpy.js` refuses
     them). A reactor's energy balance with acetic acid needs them, so that is part of step 3, using the same
     dimerization enthalpy.
5. **"from-equilibrium" reverse rates** need the activity basis, or kPa for a vapour reaction, where K is defined.
   For concentration or mole-fraction rates the user gives the reverse rate constant.
6. **The expression parser** ("Write your own kinetics") stays in step 9, as the maintainer decided on 2026-10-22,
   although the proposal's step 2 still lists it.

## Eley–Rideal and Mars–van Krevelen (searched, as the proposal asked)

Rawlings and Ekerdt do not give these forms. A web search on 2026-10-22 found only these:

- LibreTexts physical chemistry course pages on rate laws from Langmuir isotherms (the page returned 404 when opened);
- the Ansys Chemkin manual (a commercial program's documentation, not open);
- course notes and research articles that fit Mars–van Krevelen models to their own data, none opened with the
  general form printed under an open licence.

So neither is built in. They can be written as your own formula (step 9); a rate law from a paper that has the LHHW shape can be entered
as LHHW now.
