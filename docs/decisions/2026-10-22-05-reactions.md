# 2026-10-22: Reaction description, K(T) and rate forms (proposal 0010, step 2)

Pull request: reactions step 2 (`src/reactions/`).

## What the maintainer asked for

"can go to next" (2026-10-22), after the formation data (step 1) was merged. This is the next step of proposal 0010.

## What was done

- **The reaction description and its checks** (`checkReaction`, `checkReactions`, `reactionBalance`):
  - components are looked up by name, alias, CAS number or formula;
  - the element balance is checked against the components' formulas, and the imbalance is named;
  - the phase (vapour or liquid) sets the standard state;
  - the equilibrium is from the formation data or a cited ln K correlation (ln K = A + B/T + C ln T);
  - the rate law is checked too.
- **ΔrH°, ΔrG° and K(T)** (`reactionProperties`): from the components' formation records by the Gibbs–Helmholtz
  equation with the databank's heat capacities. Records of another state are taken to the reaction's standard state
  by stated paths:
  - ideal gas ⇄ pure liquid through the saturated vapour;
  - crystal → liquid with the melting data.
- **The rate forms** (`reactionRate`, `RATE_FORMS`): power law, reversible power law (reverse rate given, or from K),
  LHHW, Michaelis–Menten, Monod with substrate or product inhibition, and the Blackman, Tessier, Moser and Contois
  equations. Each constant can follow Arrhenius with k0 T^B. The forms are as printed in Rawlings and Ekerdt, 2nd ed.
- **Checks:** validation/python/reference_reactions.py.
  - Vapour reactions against Cantera 3.2.0, with NASA 9 polynomials fitted on 25 K ranges to the same ideal-gas heat
    capacities, and against a scipy enthalpy-and-entropy integration. Cantera and scipy agree to 8e-9 in ln K; the
    engine meets both within 1e-6 relative in K, 298–700 K.
  - Liquid reactions against the scipy route, within 1e-6 in ln K: diethyl ether from ethanol, and glucose to
    fructose from their crystal records.
  - test/reactions.test.js has 12 tests.
- Docs: [docs/models/reactions.md](../models/reactions.md).

## Choices made in this step (for the maintainer to confirm)

1. **Standard pressure 100 kPa** (1 bar, as CODATA and the measured formation records). ChemSep does not state the
   reference pressure of its Gibbs energies of formation in the fields read. If it were 1 atm, the difference would be
   at most RT ln(1.01325) = 33 J/mol per mole of gas in a reaction, 1.3 % in K at 298 K.
2. **Pure-liquid standard state at T.** The effect of pressure is neglected. The pure liquid's enthalpy and fugacity
   are taken from the mixture enthalpies themselves (a one-component system with the ideal-gas vapour), so reactions
   and flowsheet energy balances share one basis: the liquid-heat-capacity basis of #34, f_L = psat.
3. **Crystal records to the liquid** use ΔHfus at Tm with no correction for the heat capacities. This affects the
   sugars, the solid acids and levoglucosan.
4. **Dimerizing acids** (acetic acid): the maintainer, 2026-10-22: "I cannot add reactions for acetic acid? we need
   to fix this in this package". Fixed. The formation record is ChemSep's ideal-gas monomer, and it is taken to the
   liquid through the saturated vapour of monomers and dimers, with the chemical theory CHEPTA already uses
   (`src/thermo/vapour.js`). The fugacity of the saturated vapour is the monomer's partial pressure. Its enthalpy, per
   mole of acid, is y_D ΔH_dim/(y_M + 2 y_D) against the monomer, with ΔH_dim = −R ln10 · B = −60.6 kJ/mol from the
   temperature dependence of the dimerization constant (van 't Hoff; the constant's source is the association record,
   Marek and Standart as used by Schmid et al. 2007). ChemSep's heat of vaporization of acetic acid, 22.8 kJ/mol at
   298.15 K, is to this associated vapour.
   - Check against measured values: acetic acid's liquid ΔfH° from this path is −482.9 kJ/mol; the WebBook's most
     recent value is −483.5 kJ/mol (Steele et al. 1997). Its ΔfG° is −388.3 kJ/mol against −388.1 kJ/mol (with S°
     158.0 J/(mol K), Martin and Andon 1982). Ethanol, ethyl acetate and water are within 1 kJ/mol too.
   - Esterification K on the pure-liquid basis comes out at 23 at 298 K and 18 at 353 K, with ΔrH° = −5.6 kJ/mol.
   - **Acetic acid enthalpies everywhere** (the maintainer, 2026-10-22: "i cannot believe we do not have entalpies
     for acetic acid"). Until now every enthalpy of a system with acetic acid was refused, so heaters, flash drums
     with a duty and exchangers stopped. The vapour's enthalpy now adds the dimers' (ξ ΔH_dim for ξ dimers per mole,
     from the dimer equilibrium at T and P), and the liquid's anchor uses the dimers of the pure acid's saturated
     vapour. This holds in `pure()`, in every activity model (NRTL, UNIQUAC, Mod. UNIFAC 2.0, ideal) and in the
     flowsheet. The same holds with the chemical theory switched off for the phase equilibrium, because ChemSep's
     heat of vaporization is to the dimerized vapour.
   - Checked against the independent Python reference (`reference_enthalpy.py`, the dimer split by brentq and the
     dimerization enthalpy by a numerical derivative of K). A flowsheet test heats and partly evaporates acetic acid in
     water with NRTL and with Mod. UNIFAC 2.0.
   - A gap that is stated, not hidden: the liquid follows ChemSep's liquid heat capacity, and the vapour its monomer
     heat capacity and dimers. So the pure acid's h_V − h_L equals ChemSep's ΔHvap at 298.15 K but is 21.4 against
     23.5 kJ/mol (8.7 % low) at the normal boiling point: ChemSep's records and the dimerization constant are not
     fully consistent over that range. The test pins the gap.
5. **"from-equilibrium" reverse rates** need the activity basis, or kPa for a vapour reaction, where K is defined.
   For concentration or mole-fraction rates the user gives the reverse rate constant.
6. **The expression parser** ("Write your own kinetics") stays in step 9, as the maintainer decided on 2026-10-22,
   although the proposal's step 2 still lists it.

## Other acids (the maintainer, 2026-10-22: "is this problem similar for other acids like adipic, fumaric and so on?")

Every acid in the databank was checked:

- **Solids at 25 °C with a vapour pressure** (adipic, benzoic, salicylic, palmitic, stearic acid). Their liquid
  records start at the melting point, and every liquid enthalpy was anchored at 298.15 K, so their liquid enthalpies
  were refused in flowsheets and reactions. **Fixed:** the liquid is anchored where its records start,
  h_L(Ta) = h_IG(Ta) + h_R,sat(Ta) − ΔHvap(Ta), then follows cp_L. Components whose records cover 298.15 K do not
  change. The Python reference anchors the same way (adipic and benzoic acid cases), and a flowsheet test heats
  adipic acid with water. Below Ta the liquid is refused with the reason, as before.
- **Other acids that dimerize in the vapour** (formic, propionic, acrylic and others). CHEPTA has no dimerization
  constant for them, so their vapour is a monomer ideal gas. Their liquid ΔfH° from ChemSep's ideal-gas record is
  then off the measured liquid values of the WebBook:

  | Acid | CHEPTA (kJ/mol) | WebBook (kJ/mol) |
  |---|---|---|
  | formic acid | −413.6 | −425.1 (Guthrie 1974) |
  | propionic acid | −484.3 | −510.8 (Lebedeva 1964) |
  | acrylic acid | −365.4 | −377.8 to −383.8 |

  Acetic acid, with its dimers, is within 0.6 kJ/mol. Their heats of reaction are off by as much, and their vapour's
  enthalpy misses the dimers. The latent heat at saturation is ChemSep's ΔHvap, so plain heating and evaporation
  duties are near right. **To fix:** dimerization constants from open sources, added as `association` records like
  acetic acid's. **Searched on 2026-10-22 (the maintainer: "yes look for the pairing constants"), no open data:**
  - the NIST WebBook reaction thermochemistry of formic, acetic, propionic, acrylic and butyric acid (ion clusters
    only, no neutral dimer);
  - the ThermoML Archive (ThermoML.v2020-09-30), every file that names one of these acids with an equilibrium or
    association constant (25 files: none is a gas-phase dimerization);
  - the open literature. Chen, Brooks and Scheraga, J. Phys. Chem. B 112 (2008) 242 (PMC2561919) simulates dimers in
    water, so it has no measured gas-phase values. A 2025 Fluid Phase Equilib. study is computed (Monte Carlo), and
    computed values are not data.
  - The measurements found are not open:
    - two J. Chem. Soc. A articles of 1969 on the gas-phase dimerization enthalpies of formic, acetic and propionic
      acid (doi:10.1039/J19690002975, 10.1039/J19690002203: no free copy known to Unpaywall);
    - J. Marek's series in Collect. Czech. Chem. Commun. (1955), from which acetic acid's constant comes
      (doi:10.1135/cccc19551490: sold by the archive, not free).

  Their records stay as they are; a cited constant can be added once an open copy is found.
- **Nonvolatile acids** (fumaric, succinic, citric, lactic, itaconic, malic and the others). They stay in the
  liquid, so there is no vapour or dimer problem: their liquid enthalpy is the integral of their liquid heat capacity.
  In reactions:
  - succinic, citric and lactic acid work, from their crystal records with the melting data;
  - fumaric and itaconic acid have a crystal record but no open enthalpy of fusion, so they cannot be taken to the
    liquid. Searched again on 2026-10-22 (the maintainer: "look also for fumaric and itaconic data"):
    - itaconic acid has a measured melting temperature, 440.15 ± 1.06 K (Yang et al., Fluid Phase Equilib. 314 (2012)
      180, ThermoML Archive), but no enthalpy of fusion anywhere open (the ThermoML Archive's other itaconic file is
      about its monoesters, Richard et al. 2016);
    - fumaric acid has melting temperatures on the WebBook (about 560-563 K), but no measured enthalpy of fusion;
    - the only value found is a Joback estimate (Cheméo), which is not data;
  - malic, glycolic, glucaric, 3-hydroxypropionic acid, FDCA, diphenolic and muconic acid have no open enthalpy of
    formation ([MISSING_PROPERTIES_2026-10-22.md](../MISSING_PROPERTIES_2026-10-22.md));
  - terephthalic acid has ChemSep's ideal-gas record, but CHEPTA gives it no vapour pressure, so there is no path to
    the liquid.

## Eley–Rideal and Mars–van Krevelen (searched, as the proposal asked)

Rawlings and Ekerdt do not give these forms. A web search on 2026-10-22 found only these:

- LibreTexts physical chemistry course pages on rate laws from Langmuir isotherms (the page returned 404 when opened);
- the Ansys Chemkin manual (a commercial program's documentation, not open);
- course notes and research articles that fit Mars–van Krevelen models to their own data, none opened with the
  general form printed under an open licence.

So neither is built in. They can be written as your own formula (step 9); a rate law from a paper that has the LHHW shape can be entered
as LHHW now.
